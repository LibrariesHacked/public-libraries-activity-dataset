Library services supply these figures to [Arts Council England](https://www.artscouncil.org.uk/supporting-arts-museums-and-libraries/supporting-libraries). Some returns have mistakes, gaps or changes in how activity was counted. An unusual figure is not necessarily wrong.

## What a note means

* **Worth checking:** A figure looks unusual or does not agree with another part of the return. It remains as reported, unless a separate correction is recorded.
* **Corrected:** An estimated correction is available. The original figure is kept too; the note explains what changed and why.
* **Excluded:** An affected figure is left out of totals because it cannot reasonably be used. The reported value remains in the audit evidence.
* **Spread across months:** A quarterly or annual figure has been divided evenly across its months. These monthly values are not separate observations.

A service closure, a changed counting method or an estimate supplied by the service can explain a large change. Relevant explanations are kept with the evidence. Missing data do not mean no activity, and a note does not mean the whole service's return is wrong.

The same measure can include a correction alongside other figures that are still worth checking.

## What the figures mean

* **Active users:** People who borrowed at least one physical or digital item during the financial year, not everyone with a library card.
* **Loans:** Items issued. Loan age groups describe the intended audience of the items, not necessarily the age of the borrower.
* **Events and attendance:** Events are sessions held; attendance counts visits to those sessions. One person can attend several times.
* **Visits and outreach:** Visits count entries to library sites. Mobile and home-delivery figures may count interactions or use estimates, depending on the return.
* **Computer hours and Wi-Fi:** Computer use is measured in hours. Wi-Fi counts sessions, not individual people.
* **Financial year:** April to March. An unavailable figure is not the same as a reported zero.
